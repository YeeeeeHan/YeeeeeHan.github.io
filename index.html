ArtAgain

ArtAgain is an art resale business. I have worked with them independently as a consulting engineer since March 2026. They asked for an AI valuation engine to reduce the research behind each price, and for an AR tool that lets buyers see an artwork on their own wall.

My first valuation prototype was an AI agent that searched the web for similar works and proposed a price. The results were unreliable, because I had chosen the approach before understanding the task. I then asked the staff member who does the pricing to walk me through her process. She opens one auction database, looks up the artist, and filters by medium, edition, size and year until a few comparables remain. None of those steps needs a model. The work is repetitive, and the repetition was what the team wanted removed.

The second version uses explicit filters, with a short note explaining why each comparable matched, and she still sets the price. AI is used for one task. The database lists every auction result for an artist, so past sales of the work being priced are mixed with sales of the artist's other works. Rules can flag likely matches by title and size, but confirming that two records show the same piece requires comparing the photos, and that comparison is the one step handed to a model.

My takeaway is to observe how the work is done before choosing the technology, to use rules for routine steps, and to keep AI for the questions rules cannot answer.

Pricing was one part of a longer engagement. I mapped how a seller gets from a first WhatsApp message to a live Shopify listing and found steps that existed only in chat messages and in people's memory. I then built the database, the staff's views and a listing workspace connected to Shopify, and refined each version with the staff on real seller submissions. Listing an artwork went from three days to two hours.

I also built a "view on your wall" page for iPhone. A buyer opens a listing's link, taps one button and sees the artwork on their own wall at its recorded size, with no app to install. It reads the photo and size from the Shopify feed.

Compliance screening for a Singapore financial institution

I engaged the compliance team of a Singapore financial institution independently. Their screening of every party in a deal was manual. Each name got its own Google search, the evidence was saved to files, and the same names were entered again into a separate monitoring system.

I built a working demo of that workflow and took it to the team, asking them to correct anything that did not match how they work. It covered a screening run for each party with an evidence summary, the reviewer's recorded outcome and reason for every result, a full run history for audit, risk ratings that set each party's next review date, and daily monitoring.

In a later discovery session the team walked me through their web screening step by step, and the scope narrowed to that piece and its audit trail. The compliance team is championing it internally.

I audited our own SDK in January 2026. My notes asked what it did that LangGraph didn't already make easy. I proposed a core of four dependencies in place of more than a hundred, with tools that work in any agent framework. The migration was deferred.
